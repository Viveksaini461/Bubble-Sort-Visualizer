Home Workout Blueprint: 4-Week Plan with No Equipment

By David Ross, CPT · Certified Personal Trainer · July 20, 2025 · 11 min read

You can gain strength, mobility, and confidence at home without equipment. The key is smart exercise selection, gradual progression, and consistency. This 4‑week plan uses simple movement patterns—push, pull, hinge, squat, carry—and builds a foundation that you can maintain forever. Sessions take 20–30 minutes and require only floor space and a chair.

Weekly structure

Train 3 days per week (e.g., Mon/Wed/Fri) with optional 2 short mobility sessions. Each workout includes a warm‑up, strength circuit, and brief finisher. Rest 45–75 seconds between sets. Move with control—full range beats speed.

Movement standards

  • Squat: Hips below parallel if comfortable; keep knees tracking over toes.
  • Hinge: Hips back, neutral spine, feel stretch in hamstrings.
  • Push: Hands under shoulders, ribs down, full lockout.
  • Pull: Squeeze shoulder blades; use towel rows under a door if needed.
  • Carry: Brace core, walk tall; substitute a loaded backpack if available.

4-week plan

Warm‑up (every session): 2 minutes brisk marching, 10 arm circles each way, 10 hip hinges, 10 squats, 20‑second plank.

Week 1–2 (2–3 rounds):

  • Bodyweight squats × 10–12
  • Incline push‑ups (hands on table) × 8–10
  • Hip hinge to chair × 12
  • Towel row under door × 8–10
  • Side plank × 15–20 sec/side

Week 3–4 (3–4 rounds):

  • Split squats × 8–10/side
  • Push‑ups on floor × 6–10 (elevate hands if needed)
  • Romanian deadlift with backpack × 10–12
  • Doorframe row × 8–12
  • Hollow hold × 15–25 sec

Finishers (optional)

  • 3 rounds: 20 jumping jacks, 10 mountain climbers/side, 10 glute bridges
  • Or 10‑minute brisk walk outside

Expert tips

  • Grease the groove: Do a few reps throughout the day to accumulate volume without fatigue.
  • Tempo: Use a 3‑second lowering phase to build tension and control.
  • Progression: Add reps first, then sets, then reduce incline/assistance.
  • Mobility: Spend 5 minutes on hips and T‑spine after workouts to stay pain‑free.

FAQs

How sore should I be? Mild soreness is okay; sharp joint pain is not. Reduce reps and slow down if needed.

What if I only have 15 minutes? Do 2 rounds of the main circuit and a short walk. Consistency beats perfection.

Can I add cardio? Yes—walks, cycling, or light jogs on off days complement strength.
